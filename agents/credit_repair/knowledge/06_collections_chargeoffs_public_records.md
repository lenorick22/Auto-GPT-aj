# Collections, charge-offs, and public records

## Charge-off
- Creditor writes off the debt for accounting after prolonged delinquency (often ~180 days for cards).
- You may still owe the balance. The tradeline can remain and severely hurt payment history.
- Paying/settling does not always remove the history; it may update status to paid/settled charge-off.

## Collections
- Debt may be sold or assigned to a collector.
- Original account + collection can both appear; reporting should not be misleading/duplicative in unfair ways — review carefully.
- Ask: Is it yours? Is the amount right? Who owns it? Is it within reporting/collection limits?

## Pay for delete
- Sometimes collectors agree to delete in exchange for payment — **not guaranteed** and less common than marketing claims.
- Get any agreement **in writing** before paying. Prefer lawful negotiation; don’t rely on verbal promises.

## Settlement vs pay in full
- Settlement can reduce cash outlay but may have tax consequences (canceled debt) — suggest consulting a tax professional when relevant.
- Update goals: stop collection calls, update statuses, then rebuild.

## Bankruptcy
- Can remain on reports for years (chapter type affects timing). Exact reporting periods should be verified for the user’s case.
- During/after bankruptcy, follow attorney advice; don’t freestyle aggressive disputes that conflict with court process.

## Statutes of limitations vs reporting periods
- **SOL** (lawsuit time limit) ≠ **credit reporting time limit**. A debt can be unpaid, uncollectible via lawsuit, and still report — or vice versa depending on facts/state law.
- Never tell a user a debt is legally dead without jurisdiction-specific analysis — point them to a consumer attorney or legal aid when SOL is the question.

## Aging of negatives
Many negatives age off around seven years from the date of first delinquency for that episode (details vary). Confirm current FCRA guidance rather than guessing dates for the user.
