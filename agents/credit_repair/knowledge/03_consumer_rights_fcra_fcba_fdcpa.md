# Consumer rights primer (US) — FCRA / FCBA / FDCPA

*Educational summary, not legal advice. Re-check CFPB/FTC for current details.*

## FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act)
- Right to dispute inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable information with bureaus and furnishers.
- Bureaus generally must investigate within about **30 days** (can extend in limited cases).
- Right to free annual reports; recent policy has allowed more frequent free reports via AnnualCreditReport.com — **re-verify**.
- Security freezes and fraud alerts available.
- Adverse action notices when credit reports are used to deny credit.

## Direct disputes to furnishers
You can dispute with the company that reported the item, not only the bureau. Keep copies and dates.

## FCBA (Fair Credit Billing Act)
- Applies mainly to **open-end credit** (e.g., credit cards) billing errors.
- Timely written billing-error notices can trigger investigation duties.
- Useful when a card statement has unauthorized charges or billing mistakes.

## FDCPA (Fair Debt Collection Practices Act)
- Governs many **third-party collectors** (rules for original creditors can differ).
- Limits harassment, false statements, unfair practices.
- Consumers may request validation / certain communication limits.
- Does not erase a valid debt by itself.

## Medical and other special categories
Rules around medical debt reporting have evolved — confirm current CFPB/bureau guidance before advising.

## Enforcement / help
- CFPB complaint portal
- State attorney general / consumer protection
- Nonprofit credit counselors (check NFCC.org style referrals)
- Licensed consumer attorney for lawsuits or complex disputes
